Methodology

What this data can and can't tell you.

Earnings & debt figures — the biggest caveat

Median earnings and debt figures come from the US Department of Education's College Scorecard, which builds them from federal financial-aid and tax records tied to Title IV aid recipients. International students are generally not eligible for Title IV aid, so they're largely not part of the population these numbers are calculated from. If you're an international applicant, treat the earnings figure on this site as a rough signal about a school's graduates broadly — not a specific prediction for someone in your situation.

Earnings are school-wide, not by major

A school's median earnings figure blends every graduate together — a Computer Science graduate and an Art History graduate from the same school are averaged into one number. It doesn't tell you what graduates of the specific major you're interested in went on to earn.

CDS data is self-reported and incomplete

Test-score ranges, admission strategy (Early Decision/Action, waitlist), and merit aid figures come from each school's own published Common Data Set (CDS) — a real document schools fill out themselves, not independently audited federal data. Not every school publishes one, and not every field is filled in by every school that does. Roughly a third of the schools in this catalog currently have CDS data on file; the rest show a plain "not available" note rather than a guess.

What isn't covered at all

Nothing on this site captures TOEFL/IELTS score requirements, international-specific scholarships, or a school's need-blind vs. need-aware policy for international applicants specifically. These aren't part of any structured federal or CDS dataset — they exist only as free text on each school's own international-admissions webpage, so they can't be pulled in automatically the way the rest of this site's data is.

Admission rate is a blunt instrument

A school's overall admission rate doesn't distinguish between holistic review and stats-first review, doesn't account for how much test scores actually factor into decisions post-test-optional trends, and doesn't separate international applicant pools (which are frequently more competitive than the overall published rate) from domestic ones. The "where you stand" comparator on each school's page shows where your test score falls in the reported range — it is not a prediction of your admission odds.

STEM OPT eligibility badges

The "STEM" badge on a major only reflects the four CIP families the law designates as core STEM (Engineering, Biological/Biomedical Sciences, Mathematics/Statistics, Physical Sciences) — confirmed with certainty. Many other majors, including Computer Science, are also STEM OPT eligible under DHS's separate "related fields" list, but that list approves specific majors one at a time rather than a whole subject area, and isn't reflected here. A major without the badge may still qualify — check the official DHS list before ruling anything out.

Sources

Tuition, admissions, outcomes, majors, and location: US Dept. of Education College Scorecard. Test scores, admission strategy, merit aid: school-published Common Data Set, via CollegeData.FYI.
